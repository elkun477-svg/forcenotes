Salesforce Is Buying Cresta to Give Agentforce a Phone Voice

Salesforce's shopping trip isn't over. Its latest pick is Cresta, a company whose AI agents answer the phone and coach the humans who still do.

On November 2, 2026, Salesforce announced a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta. That phrase means both sides have signed, but the deal still has to close, usually after regulators and shareholders sign off. Salesforce didn't share the price; The Information reported it at around $2.7 billion.

So what does Cresta do? Two things. Its voice agents handle a customer call from start to finish: they listen, understand the request and take action, like rebooking a flight or updating an address. Its second product watches live calls handled by human reps and offers real-time coaching, such as suggesting the next step or flagging a missed compliance line. Brands like Marriott, Comcast and Alaska Airlines already use it.

Salesforce plans to fold Cresta into Service Cloud (its customer service product) and Agentforce. The pitch is a neat division of labour: Salesforce knows the customer's history, Cresta knows how to hold the conversation. Salesforce also says Cresta will keep supporting the contact centre and phone systems its customers use today, so nobody has to rip out their telephony overnight.

This is the third acquisition announcement in roughly two weeks, after Braze and Rune Technologies. At this rate, Salesforce's corporate development team deserves its own Agentforce agent.

Why this matters if you're learning Salesforce: Service Cloud skills are getting more valuable, not less. Voice agents still need clean case data, sensible routing and good knowledge articles to work from. If you understand how cases, queues and omni-channel routing fit together, you'll be the person who makes these agents useful.
