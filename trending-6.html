Salesforce Just Bought an AI That Watches Your Dashboards For You

Salesforce signed a deal to acquire Loops, an Israeli AI startup that monitors your business metrics, figures out why they changed, and tells you what to do next. The whole thing is headed into Tableau.

On October 12, 2026, Salesforce announced it had signed a definitive agreement to acquire Loops, an AI analytics startup based in Israel. The deal, expected to close in early November, will fold Loops's technology and team into Tableau.

So what does Loops actually do? In plain terms: it watches your KPIs so you don't have to. You point it at your business metrics — things like pipeline conversion rates, support ticket volume, or revenue by region — and Loops uses machine learning to detect when something changes and then explains why it changed. Not just "conversion dropped 12%," but "conversion dropped because deals from the Mid-Market segment with contract values above $50K are stalling at the proposal stage." It's the analytical layer between a chart going the wrong direction and a decision about what to do about it.

Salesforce has been calling this direction "agentic analytics," which means moving from dashboards that describe the past to agents that proactively surface what matters and suggest next steps. Loops fits that story almost perfectly.

The integration plan is straightforward on paper: Loops goes into Tableau, and Tableau becomes smarter about flagging root causes automatically instead of making users drill down manually. Existing standalone Loops customers haven't been given a clear roadmap yet, so if you're one of them, it's worth locking in export and renewal terms before the close in November.

Why this matters if you're learning Salesforce: Tableau is already a major part of the Salesforce analytics story, and understanding how it sits alongside CRM Analytics (formerly Einstein Analytics) is a common architecture question. This acquisition signals that Salesforce wants Tableau to be more than a reporting layer — it wants it to do some of the thinking. If you're studying the integration and data tracks, keep an eye on how Loops capabilities surface in Tableau after the deal closes.
